## For future Claude
Doc about the **US federal income tax** (the one the **IRS** administers), in US dollars (USD),
verified 2026-06-19. Serves tiers 3-5. The 7-bracket structure was made permanent by the *One Big
Beautiful Bill* (OBBBA, signed 2025-07-04), but the standard-deduction amounts are inflation-adjusted
each year, so they are **volatile** — re-verify before tax year 2027. **Key differentiator:** this is
the US **federal** tax; states may levy their own income tax on top. It is **NOT** Mexico's ISR and is
**NOT** administered by the SAT — keep Mexican terms out of this doc.

## For teens (tier3-4) <!-- age_band: tier3,tier4 -->

The **federal income tax** is money that people in the United States pay to the national government on
what they **earn**: a paycheck, tips, or what you make from a small business. It is collected by an
office called the **IRS** (Internal Revenue Service), and it is paid in **dollars**.

The most important idea is that **not everyone pays the same share**. The system is **tiered**: your
income is split into bands, like the steps of a staircase. The first part of what you earn pays very
little, and only the part that reaches higher steps pays a bigger share. That is why someone who earns
more is asked for a slightly larger slice of each extra dollar, not of all their money.

Before any of that, the government lets you subtract a fixed amount that is **tax-free**, called the
**standard deduction**. It works like an automatic discount: if you earned only a little, that free
amount may cover almost everything, so you end up paying very little or nothing.

Every year, people fill out a form (the famous "1040") where they report how much they earned and how
much was already taken out of each paycheck. If too much was taken out, the IRS **refunds** the
difference; if too little, they pay what is missing. Learning this early helps you understand why your
first paycheck is smaller than the number you were promised.

## Advanced (tier5) <!-- age_band: tier5 -->

The US federal income tax is **progressive**: it is applied through **7 marginal brackets** with rates
of **10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35% and 37%**. The *One Big Beautiful Bill* (OBBBA), signed on
**July 4, 2025**, made that bracket structure **permanent**, where it previously had an expiration
date. Everything is administered by the **IRS**.

It is essential to distinguish two rates:

- **Marginal rate:** the one that applies to the **last dollar** earned (the highest bracket you
  reach). It determines what an additional dollar of income costs you.
- **Effective rate:** total tax divided by total income. It is always **lower** than the marginal
  rate, because the first brackets were taxed at low rates. Confusing the two makes people believe
  that "moving up a bracket" penalizes their whole salary, which is false: only the money inside the
  new bracket pays the new rate.

The calculation starts from **AGI** (*adjusted gross income*): total income minus certain adjustments.
From there you subtract the **standard deduction** to reach taxable income. For tax year **2026**, the
standard deduction is **$16,100** for single filers
<!-- @fact id=us.std_deduction.single value=16,100 verified=2026-06-19 src=src_irs_2026_inflation volatility=high -->
and **$32,200** for married couples filing jointly.
<!-- @fact id=us.std_deduction.mfj value=32,200 verified=2026-06-19 src=src_irs_2026_inflation volatility=high -->

> **US vs MX differentiator:** this is the US **federal** income tax, in USD, administered by the
> **IRS**. **States** may levy their own income tax **on top** of the federal one (with their own
> rates and rules). It is **not** Mexico's ISR and is **not** administered by the SAT; these are
> separate systems and must not be mixed.
