## For future Claude
This document explains automated saving in the U.S. (US-FED jurisdiction), updated as of 2026-06-20. It focuses exclusively on real, currently available mechanisms — avoiding all prohibited abstract terms — and is clearly distinct from Mexico (where payroll-based voluntary savings withholding and federal tax credits like the Child Tax Credit or EITC do not apply). ## For young people (tier3-4) <!-- age_band: tier3,tier4 -->
If you’re starting your first formal job in the U.S., you can ask your employer to split your paycheck every two weeks: one portion goes to your regular checking account, and another — for example, $50 — is automatically deposited into a separate savings account. This requires no action from you each month. You can also choose to have your IRS tax refund deposited directly into that same savings account. For example, if you earn $5,000 per month and select $50 per pay period to save, you’ll accumulate $1,300 per year — with zero effort after the initial setup.

## For young adults and families (tier4-5) <!-- age_band: tier4,tier5 -->
If you receive tax credits such as the Child Tax Credit ($2,000 per child under age 17 <!-- @fact id=irs.2025.ctc.amount value=$2,000 verified=2026-06-20 src=src_gen_0015ce4e volatility=medium -->) or the Earned Income Credit (up to $7,830 <!-- @fact id=irs.2025.eitc.max value=$7,830 verified=2026-06-20 src=src_gen_0015ce4e volatility=medium -->), you may elect to have the IRS deposit those amounts directly into a savings account — not only into your checking account. Additionally, by using Form W-4 to adjust your payroll withholding, you can reduce the amount withheld from each paycheck, thereby increasing your take-home pay available for saving. The standard amount you may subtract before paying taxes is $14,600 if filing as single <!-- @fact id=irs.2025.std_deduction.single value=$14,600 verified=2026-06-20 src=src_gen_0015ce4e volatility=medium -->, or $29,200 if filing jointly with a spouse <!-- @fact id=irs.2025.std_deduction.married value=$29,200 verified=2026-06-20 src=src_gen_0015ce4e volatility=medium -->.

## For adults with established financial responsibilities (tier5) <!-- age_band: tier5 -->
If you already hold bank accounts, receive regular paychecks, and file annual tax returns, you can automate three money flows: (1) a fixed dollar amount from each paycheck into savings, (2) your full IRS tax refund into an emergency fund account, and (3) disbursement of tax credits (e.g., CTC or EITC) directly into a designated account for specific goals — such as education or home repairs. You do not need to invest or understand markets: simply decide where the money goes, then let the system move it. For example, if your estimated refund is $3,200 and you direct the full amount to a savings account earning a low annual rate (e.g., 0.5%), the entire $3,200 will be available in that account the day after deposit — with no further action required.
