## For future Claude
Doc about Mexico's **ISR** (*Impuesto Sobre la Renta*), the tax on what you **earn**, administered by
the **SAT**, federal jurisdiction, verified 2026-06-19. Serves tiers 3-5. Individual rates are
**progressive** and the peso brackets are **adjusted for inflation** (volatile: re-verify yearly); the
company rate (30%) is more stable. Do NOT confuse it with the US income tax or the IRS: ISR is Mexican,
its brackets are in **pesos**, and there are no forms like the 1040 or W-2 here.

## For teens (tier3-4) <!-- age_band: tier3,tier4 -->

The **ISR** (*Impuesto Sobre la Renta*, income tax) is the tax you pay on the money you **earn**: your
salary if you work, what you charge for a service, or the earnings of a small business. While VAT is
paid when you **buy**, ISR is paid when you **earn**. The one who administers and collects it is the
**SAT** (the Tax Administration Service).

The key idea is that ISR is **progressive**: the **more you earn, the larger the share** you contribute.
Someone who earns little contributes a small part; someone who earns a lot contributes a bigger part.
This way the system tries to be fairer, because contributing does not weigh the same on someone who can
barely make ends meet as on someone who has plenty to spare.

Who files, and when? **Individuals** (you, as a person) file their annual return in **April**, and
**companies** do so in **March**. On top of that, throughout the year there are **monthly provisional
payments**: advances so the whole tax does not pile up until the end. If you work for a company, it
usually **withholds** your ISR from each paycheck and hands it to the SAT for you, just like a store
collects VAT. So when you grow up and work, part of your salary will already be set aside for ISR
before it reaches your hands.

## Advanced (tier5) <!-- age_band: tier5 -->

**ISR** is a **direct** tax levied on the income of **individuals** (*personas físicas*) and
**companies** (*personas morales*), governed by the **Income Tax Law** (*Ley del Impuesto Sobre la
Renta*) and administered by the SAT.

- **Individuals:** a **progressive bracketed schedule** set in pesos applies. Each bracket has a **fixed
  fee** plus a **marginal rate** applied only to the amount above the lower limit, so your effective
  rate is always lower than your marginal rate. The brackets are **adjusted for inflation**, so they
  change over time.
- **Companies:** a general rate of **30%** on taxable profit. <!-- @fact id=mx.isr.pm value=30% verified=2026-06-19 src=src_lisr volatility=medium -->
- **Personal deductions:** expenses such as medical, hospital and funeral fees, real interest on
  mortgage loans, tuition (where a stimulus applies) and retirement contributions **reduce the taxable
  base**, within the caps the law sets.

**Annual mechanics:** individuals file an annual return in **April** and companies in **March**,
crediting the **monthly provisional payments** already made; the difference yields a balance payable or
a credit balance. Associated obligations: registration in the **RFC**, issuing **CFDI**, and keeping
accounting records where required. Regimes such as **RESICO** simplify the calculation for
lower-income taxpayers.

> **MX vs US differentiator:** **ISR** is the **Mexican** income tax, administered by the **SAT**, with
> brackets set in **pesos** and adjusted for inflation. It is **not** the US federal income tax, it is
> **not** administered by the **IRS**, and there are **no** forms like the **1040** or **W-2** here.
> They are different systems; do not mix them.
