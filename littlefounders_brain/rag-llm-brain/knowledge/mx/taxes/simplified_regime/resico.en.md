## For future Claude
Doc about Mexico's **RESICO** (Régimen Simplificado de Confianza / Simplified Trust Regime), verified
2026-06-19, federal jurisdiction. Serves tiers 4-5. Rates and income caps are **volatile** (they
change with the annual tax miscellany and reforms) — re-verify before the review_due. **Key
differentiator:** RESICO is a Mexican **low-rate, gross-receipts** regime; it is NOT a US S-corp, LLC,
or pass-through election. Never equate it to US structures.

## For teens (tier4) <!-- age_band: tier4 -->

When someone earns money on their own —selling desserts, tutoring, coding— the government asks them to
register and choose a **tax regime**, which is like the "plan" under which they will pay their taxes.
**RESICO** (Régimen Simplificado de Confianza, the Simplified Trust Regime) is a plan designed so that
people who earn relatively little can pay in a **simple and cheap** way.

Why "trust"? Because the SAT trusts that you honestly report what you earned, and in exchange it
charges you a very low rate and cuts your paperwork. Instead of doing complicated math subtracting all
your expenses, in RESICO you pay a small part of **everything you charged** and that's it.

To enter RESICO as an individual there is a limit: you cannot earn more than **3.5 million pesos a
year**. <!-- @fact id=mx.resico.pf.limit value=3.5M verified=2026-06-19 src=src_sat_resico volatility=high -->
If you earn more than that, you no longer qualify and must move to a normal regime.

Even though RESICO is easier, it is not "free or invisible": you still have to hand over an
**electronic invoice (CFDI)** every time you get paid, and you still report your income **every month**
to the SAT. The idea is to teach you to keep your numbers from a young age, but without drowning you
in red tape.

## Advanced (tier5) <!-- age_band: tier5 -->

**RESICO** is an optional regime regulated in the **Income Tax Law (Ley del Impuesto Sobre la Renta,
LISR)** whose distinctive mechanic is taxing **gross income actually collected**, with no deductions,
at very low progressive rates. It replaces the complexity of the general regime (where income tax is
computed on profit: income minus authorized deductions) with a direct calculation on what is invoiced
and collected.

**Individual (persona física, PF).** Income tax is determined by applying a rate ranging from
**1% to 2.5%** on monthly gross income, by bracket.
<!-- @fact id=mx.resico.pf.rate value=1%-2.5% verified=2026-06-19 src=src_sat_resico volatility=high -->
The condition to stay in is not exceeding **3.5 million pesos** in annual income.
<!-- @fact id=mx.resico.pf.limit value=3.5M verified=2026-06-19 src=src_sat_resico volatility=high -->
Excluded are company partners/shareholders, foreign residents, and those earning income from certain
incompatible regimes.

**Entity (persona moral, PM).** Companies may be taxed under RESICO if their income does not exceed
**35 million pesos a year**
<!-- @fact id=mx.resico.pm.limit value=35M verified=2026-06-19 src=src_sat_resico volatility=high -->
and if they are composed **solely of individuals resident in Mexico** as partners or shareholders. The
entity accrues on a cash-flow basis (what is actually collected and paid), which simplifies
bookkeeping relative to the general regime.

Despite the simplification, formal obligations remain: registration in the **RFC**, issuance of a
**CFDI** for each transaction, **monthly** returns, and the corresponding annual return.
Non-compliance (not invoicing, not filing) can trigger **expulsion from the regime**.

> **MX vs US differentiator:** RESICO is a **Mexican** low-rate regime on **gross income**. It is
> **not** a US *S-corp*, *LLC*, or *pass-through election*: those are US legal/tax structures with a
> different logic (taxation "passes through" to the owners). Do not equate them or carry their rules
> over to RESICO.
