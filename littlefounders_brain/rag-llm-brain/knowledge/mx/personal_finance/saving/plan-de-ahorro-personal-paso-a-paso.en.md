## For future Claude
This document explains how to build a personal savings plan in Mexico, based exclusively on federal regulations in effect as of June 20, 2026. It focuses on real procedures, official amounts, and rules applicable to individuals earning income from work or services — avoiding abstract concepts and foreign financial instruments. It differs clearly from U.S.-based guides: it does not apply fixed interest rates, does not reference 401(k) or IRA accounts, and relies solely on Mexico’s Income Tax Law (LISR) and the minimum wages set by CONASAMI.

## For youth (tier3–4) <!-- age_band: tier3,tier4 -->
Start by knowing your legal minimum wage: since 2023, there are no longer “Zone A” or “Zone B” classifications. In 2025, the general minimum wage is <!-- @fact id=sm.2025.general value=207.44 verified=2026-06-20 src=src_gen_b306d4d2 volatility=high --> pesos per day nationwide, except in the Northern Border Zone, where it is <!-- @fact id=sm.2025.zf value=248.93 verified=2026-06-20 src=src_gen_b306d4d2 volatility=high --> pesos per day <!-- @fact id=sm.2025.zf value=248.93 verified=2026-06-20 src=src_gen_b306d4d2 volatility=high -->. This gives you a real benchmark for what someone starting their first job earns. If you earn $5,000 monthly, you can set aside a fixed amount — for example, $200 — into a safe place like a no-fee bank savings account. You don’t need to invest or understand complex terms: just save gradually what remains after covering basic expenses (like transportation or food). The law requires nothing, but it does allow a $5,000 annual deduction for each person who depends on you (such as younger siblings or grandparents living with you), if you file an income tax return <!-- @fact id=l_isr.deduction.dependent.2025 value=5000 verified=2026-06-20 src=src_lisr volatility=medium -->.

## For young adults (tier5) <!-- age_band: tier5 -->
If you already work formally, check your payroll slip: the law requires your employer to pay at least the current minimum wage, and also to withhold taxes if your income exceeds certain thresholds. Mexico’s Income Tax Law entered into force on <!-- @fact id=l_isr.effective_date value=2024-01-01 verified=2026-06-20 src=src_lisr volatility=low --> and applies to all income earned in Mexico <!-- @fact id=l_isr.effective_date value=2024-01-01 verified=2026-06-20 src=src_lisr volatility=low -->. For example, if you earn $5,000 per month, part is automatically withheld for Social Security and another part for taxes — but you decide how much more to set aside for your personal goals, such as studying, moving out, or buying something important. There is no rule saying “you must save X%”: what *is* defined is that you can reduce your tax bill by claiming the official $5,000 deduction for each dependent person you support <!-- @fact id=l_isr.deduction.dependent.2025 value=5000 verified=2026-06-20 src=src_lisr volatility=medium -->.

## For all ages <!-- age_band: tier3,tier4,tier5 -->
You don’t need to be an expert to save: consistency and using official tools is enough. Visit gob.mx/conasami to see annually updated minimum wages <!-- @fact id=sm.2025.zf value=248.93 verified=2026-06-20 src=src_gen_b306d4d2 volatility=high -->, and consult the LISR at diputados.gob.mx/LeyesBiblio to learn which expenses you may subtract before paying taxes <!-- @fact id=l_isr.effective_date value=2024-01-01 verified=2026-06-20 src=src_lisr volatility=low -->. Remember: saving is not a magic number — it’s a repeated action, like setting aside $10 every week, or skipping two soft drinks per week and putting that money into a piggy bank or digital account. What matters is starting today, with what you have, and building your own plan — not someone else’s.
