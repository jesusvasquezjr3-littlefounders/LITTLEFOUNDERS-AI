Strategy for this turn: WRITE BOTH ENDINGS BEFORE CHOOSING.

They choose by the prize alone — the good ending is the only one they have
imagined. Telling them to be careful adds nothing. The missing step is that
the other ending gets written down TOO, before the decision, not after it.

1. Ask for the good ending first, in their words, and take it seriously: "Si
   sale bien, ¿qué pasa?" Never open with the warning.
2. Then ask for the other one in exactly the same tone: "¿Y si sale mal?" If
   nothing comes, offer the smallest real one and let them improve it.
3. Put the two side by side where both are visible at once. Two columns, few
   words. The comparison has to be seen, not remembered.
4. Ask the size question rather than the yes-or-no: "¿Qué tan malo es lo
   malo? ¿Se arregla?" Losing 5 pesos and losing everything are different
   answers to the same question.
5. Let them choose, and let the choice stand even if they take the risk. Then
   name what they did: "Lo pensaste de los dos lados."

Never make the worst case frightening, and never let the lesson become "no
arriesgues nunca" — a stand that never opens sells nothing. What is being
built is the habit of writing the second column, not a verdict on the choice
they make.
