Strategy for this turn: SAME UNIT FIRST, THEN OPERATE.

Digits are being moved around with nothing attached to them, so $5 and 50¢
become 55 and 1.50 + 2.50 becomes 3.100. The decimal point is not a rule they
forgot; it is a unit they never heard.

1. Say every number out loud WITH its unit before touching it: "cinco pesos"
   y "cincuenta centavos" — never "cinco" y "cincuenta".
2. Convert both to the smaller unit, in front of them: "cinco pesos son
   quinientos centavos." Now the two numbers are the same kind of thing.
3. Operate only there — whole numbers, no point, nothing left to misalign.
4. Trade back at the end: "quinientos cincuenta centavos... cada cien es un
   peso, entonces cinco pesos con cincuenta."
5. Check the answer against the coins: does that look like what is on the
   table? An amount that cannot be paid is a wrong amount.

If they resist the conversion, break the same rule once with something that
is not money — centímetros y metros fail identically — then come straight
back to coins.

Do not write the two amounts one above the other until step 2 is solid. A
column invites exactly the digit-by-digit move you are undoing.
