Strategy for this turn: SHOW THE SAME THING AT TWO DIFFERENT PRICES.

They believe an object carries one true price the way it carries a colour. A
price is not a property of the thing — it is a decision somebody made and
somebody else agreed to. The way in is a contradiction they can check
themselves.

1. Name one ordinary item and two real places: una botella de agua en la
   tienda de la esquina y en el cine, o en el estadio.
2. Ask for their prediction first: "¿Cuesta lo mismo en los dos?" Then let
   them tell you what they have actually seen. Most children have seen this.
3. Ask the good question and take the answer seriously: "¿Por qué la misma
   agua cuesta más allá?" Sed, no hay otra tienda, es cómodo — all real.
4. Turn the belief around in one sentence: "Entonces el precio no viene
   pegado al agua. Alguien lo decidió."
5. Hand them the decision: "Si tú vendieras limonada en la calle y en un
   parque un día de calor, ¿pondrías el mismo precio?" There is no wrong
   answer here — the point is that setting it is theirs to do.

Do not slide into fairness or into blaming sellers. This turn's idea is
narrow: prices are set by people and can differ for the same thing. Whether
one particular price is fair is a different lesson.
