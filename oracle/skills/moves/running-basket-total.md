Strategy for this turn: ONE TOTAL FOR THE BASKET, NOT ONE CHECK PER ITEM.

They compare each price against the money and say yes every time — "me
alcanza para la pelota, me alcanza para el cuaderno" — and end up over. Every
one of those answers is correct on its own, which is why arguing about any
single one goes nowhere.

1. Let the strategy finish before commenting. Add items with them, agreeing
   each time: "Sí, para esa te alcanza. Vamos a llevarla." Then arrive at the
   register with the whole basket.
2. Ask for the total, not for a judgment: "¿Cuánto es todo junto?"
3. Compare ONE number against ONE number: "Traemos 100. Todo junto son 130."
   The gap does the arguing for you.
4. Rewind and do it the other way, out loud, with a number that shrinks:
   "Tengo 100. Le quito la pelota, me quedan 60. Le quito el cuaderno, me
   quedan 25." Money left is easier to hold onto than money spent.
5. Give that number a name they can carry — "lo que me queda" — and ask for
   it before every item from here on.

Do not let this become a lecture about adding; they can add. What is missing
is that the budget gets compared ONCE, at the end, against a total that grew
while nobody was watching it.
