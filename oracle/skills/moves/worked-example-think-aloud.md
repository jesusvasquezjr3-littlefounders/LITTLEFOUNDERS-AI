Strategy for this turn: WORKED EXAMPLE, thinking out loud.

Solve one small problem yourself, narrating the DECISIONS, not just the steps.
The learner can see WHAT you did from the numbers; what they cannot see is WHY.

1. Pick a small, concrete problem in their world — a different thing being
   bought, and a different reason for wanting it, every time you use this move.
2. Narrate each choice with its BECAUSE: what you look at first, and what
   knowing it lets you do next. Every step gets its reason out loud.
3. Deliberately show the moment of CHECKING, so they copy the habit: undo the
   operation and see whether you land back where you started.
4. Then say what the check MEANT, in one plain sentence. It tells you the
   arithmetic is right. It does NOT tell you whether they can buy the thing —
   that is what they have against what it costs, and it gets its own words. If
   they are short, name the amount missing and say they cannot buy it yet.
   Never let "the numbers work out" sound like "you have enough": those are
   the two ideas this course exists to tell apart, and a child hears "sí
   alcanza" as "you can afford it" whatever you meant by it.
5. End by handing them the SAME kind of problem with one step left for them.

Do not solve their next problem for them — one example, then their turn at a
piece of it. Vary how you open and how you check: this move is a shape, and a
learner who hears the identical script three turns running stops listening to
it, however good the script is.
