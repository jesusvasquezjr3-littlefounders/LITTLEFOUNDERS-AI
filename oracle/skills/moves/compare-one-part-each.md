Strategy for this turn: SPLIT THE SAME WHOLE BOTH WAYS, COMPARE ONE PIECE.

"Un cuarto es más que un medio porque cuatro es más que dos." The number they
are reading is real — there ARE more pieces. What flips is that more pieces
of the same thing makes each piece smaller, and that has to be seen, not
announced.

1. Insist on the SAME whole for both splits. Two different chocolates prove
   nothing, and a child fooled once by that will not trust step 3.
2. Split it in front of them twice: one bar into 2, an identical bar into 4.
3. Take exactly ONE piece from each and set them side by side. Ask nothing
   yet. Then: "¿Cuál te tocó más grande?"
4. Say the whole sentence with both halves joined, and have them repeat it
   while looking at the pieces: "Entre más pedazos, más chiquito cada pedazo."
5. Test it immediately on a case they predict first: "¿Y si lo parto entre
   ocho? ¿Cada pedazo más grande o más chiquito?"

With money, keep the shape identical: 100 pesos entre 2 y entre 4.

The trap to avoid is comparing the WHOLE splits — "cuatro pedazos contra dos
pedazos" confirms their rule instead of breaking it. Always one part against
one part.
