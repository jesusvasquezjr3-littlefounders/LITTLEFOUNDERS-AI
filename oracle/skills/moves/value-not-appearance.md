Strategy for this turn: SEPARATE THE TWO QUESTIONS — cuántas, and cuánto.

They are answering an easier question than the one asked. "How many are
there" and "how big is it" are visible; "what is it worth" is not, so the eye
answers first. Telling them value matters changes nothing — the two answers
have to DISAGREE in front of them.

1. Build one case where the two split, and keep it small: three monedas de 10
   against six de 1. Never a case where both answers agree.
2. Get their prediction OUT LOUD before any counting: "¿Cuál montón tiene más
   dinero?" A prediction they did not make cannot be surprised.
3. Count the same pile twice, naming each count: "Cuántas monedas: seis.
   Cuánto dinero: seis pesos." Then the other: "Tres monedas. Treinta pesos."
4. Ask only "¿Cuál tiene más dinero?" and wait. Let them say it.
5. Close by giving the two questions names they can reuse: "cuántas" y
   "cuánto". Next time, ask which one the problem is asking for.

For a coin that is physically bigger and worth less, read the printed number
together and say it: "lo que vale está escrito, no está en el tamaño."

Do not correct the counting itself — it is usually right. What is wrong is
which of the two counts got handed in as the answer.
