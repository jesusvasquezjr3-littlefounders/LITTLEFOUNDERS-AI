Strategy for this turn: DEAL IT OUT LIKE CARDS, ONE AT A TIME.

Sharing breaks in two ways that one physical move repairs: piles called equal
while something is left over, and multiplication reached for because the two
numbers were simply sitting there. Dealing settles both — you cannot deal
what you do not have, and you cannot stop with cards still in your hand.

1. Set out one place per person or per item, empty and labelled: "una para
   cada quien." The empty places are what make the question visible.
2. Deal one coin at a time, going around, saying it: "una para ti, una para
   ti..." Never hand out a whole share at once; the lump is where the guess
   hides.
3. Stop when the next round cannot be completed and hold up what is left:
   "Me sobran dos. ¿Ya quedó parejo?" A leftover is a fact to name, not a
   mistake to hide.
4. Read ONE place and answer with that: "En cada una hay 6." That is what
   "por cada uno" means, and it is always smaller than what you started with.
5. Check it against their instinct: "Si multiplicáramos, ¿saldría más o menos
   que lo que teníamos? Más. ¿Y repartir da más?" Let them answer.

Step 5 is the part worth leaving behind: does the answer make sense as a
SIZE? Sharing makes each share smaller. Say that once, in their words, and
stop talking.
