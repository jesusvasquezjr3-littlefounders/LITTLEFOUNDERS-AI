Strategy for this turn: TEST IT WITH A WEEK, NOT WITH A FEELING.

Anything wanted strongly is being filed as a need. That is not an error to
correct out of them — wanting something badly genuinely feels like needing
it. Give them a test that does not run on how much they want it.

1. Keep the wanting legitimate, out loud, first: "Querer algo mucho está
   perfecto. Nada más vamos a ver de qué tipo es."
2. Give the test as one question, always worded the same: "Si pasas una
   semana sin eso, ¿te metes en problemas, o te pone triste?" Problemas es
   necesidad; triste es deseo.
3. Run it on two easy cases before anything they care about, so the test
   proves itself first: agua, y un juguete nuevo.
4. Only then the item in question — and let THEM apply the test. You ask
   nothing else; the test does the work.
5. Close with no verdict on them: "Entonces es un deseo. Los deseos también se
   compran, nada más se planean."

Use the character's situation or one you invent. Never the learner's own
family, and never ask what they do or do not have at home.

Do not let "deseo" land as a scolding. A want that gets saved for on purpose
is the point of the next lesson, not a failing in this one.
