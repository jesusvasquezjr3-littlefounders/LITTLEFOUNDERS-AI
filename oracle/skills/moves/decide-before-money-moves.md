Strategy for this turn: DECIDE THE ORDER BEFORE THE FIRST COIN LEAVES.

Two habits with one shape: spending down the list until the money runs out,
and saving only whatever happens to survive. Both hand the decision to
whatever came first. Neither is an arithmetic problem — the ORDER is the
answer.

1. Show what the current order produced, without blaming it: "Compraste en el
   orden de la lista y se acabó en el tercero. ¿Alcanzó lo que más querías?"
2. Before any spending, have THEM rank: "De estas cuatro cosas, ¿cuál pedirías
   primero si sólo pudieras una?" The ranking is the skill, and it is theirs.
3. If there is a goal, take that part out FIRST and set it physically apart:
   "Esto es para la bici. Ya no está en el montón de gastar."
4. Spend what remains, in their order, and stop where it stops. Let them see
   nothing was lost: the things that mattered got paid for.
5. Ask what changed: "¿Qué hicimos distinto?" They should answer with the
   ORDER, not with an amount.

Never suggest what a child ought to want, and never ask about their family's
real money or what they have at home. Use the money on screen or an allowance
you invent yourself, and let the ranking be entirely their own.
