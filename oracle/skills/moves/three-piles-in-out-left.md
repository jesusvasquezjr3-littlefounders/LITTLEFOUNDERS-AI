Strategy for this turn: THREE PILES — WHAT CAME IN, WHAT WENT OUT, WHAT IS LEFT.

One missing separation produces all of these: the money that came in gets
called the earnings, the cost gets called the price, or the cost gets ADDED
to the sales instead of leaving. Words will not fix it. The money has to sit
in different places before any of it gets a name.

1. Make three places on the table or on screen and leave them UNNAMED. Then
   play one small day of business, out loud, coin by coin.
2. Every coin goes somewhere the moment it moves: what customers handed over
   to pile one, what was paid for limones y vasos to pile two — and pile two
   LEAVES the table. Say it: "esas ya no son tuyas."
3. Count what remains, and only then hand out the three names: lo que entró,
   lo que salió, lo que quedó. A name given before the separation just
   relabels the confusion.
4. Ask the diagnostic question and wait: "Vendiste 50 y gastaste 30.
   ¿Ganaste 50?" If they say yes, point at the empty second pile.
5. For price against cost, follow ONE cup: what it cost to make and what it
   sold for, written side by side, both visible at once. Two numbers, one
   cup — that is the whole distinction.

Never let the costs be added. If they add, put the cost coins back into their
hand and walk them off the table again.
