## Implementation and rationale

### The cartoon avatar and the covers, drawn by us (E.12, 07 §1, §3, §6)

The stored data does not change (OD-9): Core and the database keep accepting exactly the closed option set and the ten preset ids, and every existing avatar and cover keeps its values. Only the drawing is new.

- `rebuild/account/avatar/avatarKit.ts` mirrors the editor's closed catalogue (a parity test fails on drift with `lib/avatarOptions.ts` and `lib/coverPresets.ts`; `social:check` now also compares the rebuilt cover list with Core and the SQL guard). `resolveLook` turns any stored set into a drawable look: a value outside the catalogue (Core accepts any short alphanumeric value) or a missing part falls back to a stable default derived from the stored seed or the account id (a hash, not a random draw), never to a blank; an optional part (beard, glasses) is drawn only at 50% or more (the editor stores 0 or 100). `lookToStored` writes exactly the shape Core's `AvatarOptions` accepts (tested key by key and pattern by pattern).
- The parts are our own flat SVG drawings in four sprite files (`public/rebuild/avatar/{body,hair,face,extras}.svg`: 9 outfits, 16 hair and headwear styles with back and front layers, 8 eyes, 6 eyebrows, 6 mouths, 5 beards, 6 glasses) and the ten covers in one (`covers.svg`), each registered in the manifest as a class B `svg`, both modes, decorative, draft. The person's colours reach a part through CSS variables (`--lf-avatar-skin/hair/clothes`, 07 §3.1); ink, white and the token hues come from the page. No sprite holds a hex colour, text, raster, script or gradient (tested). `CartoonAvatar` layers the parts with `<use>` and clips them to a disc; a retired or unregistered sprite leaves an empty, marked slot with nothing standing in for the person (tested with a replaced manifest).
- The covers are flat compositions in the identity hues (primary, sky, mint, berry and their ridge and soft tones), no gradients, the accent never used (02 §4.2).
- The course badge is one registered hexagon (`badge.course.hexagon`, the collectible shape, primary with its ridge line, 02 §4.4 (3)); the course title is DOM text beside it.
- The asset gate gained two review families, `avatar-parts` and `profile-covers` (07 §7 item 2: the owner approves the first asset of each family).

## Decisions taken on the SPEC's conservative default (proposals for owner review)

1. **Our own cartoon avatar replaces the DiceBear drawing in rebuilt UI.** 07 D12 forbids illustration packs for meaningful visuals and S03's gate refuses DiceBear in `src/rebuild/`; E.12 keeps the avatar cartoon-only. The stored option sets are unchanged. Until the lane's next checkpoint rebuilds the lists and public profiles (and the Mentor lane rebuilds the stage's presence), the legacy surfaces still draw the same person with DiceBear, so one person looks different on legacy and rebuilt pages during the migration. The drawings are drafts awaiting the owner's first-asset style review (families `avatar-parts`, `profile-covers`).
2. **People's colours are drawn as stored.** Skin, hair and clothes colours are the person's choices from the closed palette, not token hues (07 §3 says token hues only). Recoloring a person's skin into brand hues would misrepresent them; proposal: record a "people palette" beside the tokens.
3. **Invite link only for adults and independent teens.** A child is never handed a public link to spread (E.1: the Tutor approves a child's connections; E.13: a handle shared off-platform is what the review guards against); a guest has no social layer.
4. **An avatar never saved** is drawn from a stable default derived from the account id, where DiceBear drew a seeded random look; such a person sees a different default than before.
5. **Covers** keep their ten ids and are redrawn as flat compositions (no gradients, 02 §1 fixed rules).
6. **Course badges**: one own hexagon for every course; a per-course badge-art family is asset work for the owner's review (the Forge PNGs are not registered assets).
7. **No Mentor row on the profile** (the mockup shows one): the Mentor choice is a real autonomy lever (B.24) that lives on the Mentor stage; adding a second entry point is the Mentor lane's call.
8. **A child keeps the password change** (A.6 mandates the email and the username; S04.3 kept it). The owner may prefer routing it through the Tutor.
9. **Where the Settings column stops**: one 44 rem reading column at every width.
10. **Design-system item for Lane 0**: in dark mode a secondary button (surface fill, no elevation in dark) is invisible on a surface card. This lane steps it to the raised surface on its own screens; the shared fix belongs in `rebuild/design`.
11. **Unfollowing a child or a private teen asks first** (W2P.2). The legacy page left at once; the undo needs a new approved request, so a mis-press costs the family a new approval. Unfollowing an adult stays one press, as before.
