## Per-surface rationale

### W2M.1a: the session layer moves, the behaviour does not

**What the legacy code did.** The live Mentor's non-UI logic lived beside the legacy chat UI in `frontend/src/tutor/`: `useTutorSocket.ts` (the Oracle websocket and its close-code reasons), `useMicrophone.ts`, `useHandsFreeTurn.ts`, `turnDetector.ts`, `segmentLock.ts`, `mic.ts` (Core's three microphone answers), `stage/phases.ts` (the phase vocabulary and `shotForPhase`), `stage/micForPhase.ts`, `tutorApi.ts` (every Core call of the Mentor) and `types.ts` (the wire types). The Lane 0 boundary kept these out of the rebuilt frontend's reach.

**What changed.** The files moved with `git mv` into `frontend/src/rebuild/mentor/session/`, with their tests (`useTutorSocket` 10, `useMicrophone` 14, `useHandsFreeTurn` 14, `turnDetector` 15, `segmentLock` 6, `mic` 9, `micForPhase` 13, `shotForPhase` 8). The only edits are the imports the boundary refuses:

- the character, emotion, action and shot unions (`components/characters/control/types.ts`, `tutor-scene/shots.ts`) are written out in `session/vocabulary.ts`; `tutor-scene/__tests__/rebuildMentorParity.test.ts` compares every list with the engine's own, and the stage bridge hands the values to the renderer, so a drift fails to compile or test;
- `tutorApi.ts` calls Core through `session/coreApi.ts`, a line-for-line copy of `lib/api.ts` (the same envelope handling, `resetAt` on `SESSION_LIMIT`, the same error codes);
- the microphone orb's two type unions moved into `micForPhase.ts`; the legacy `tutor/hud/MicOrb.tsx` re-exports them.

Every legacy path (`tutor/useTutorSocket.ts`, `tutor/types.ts`, `tutor/stage/phases.ts`, …) is now a one-line re-export, so the legacy Mentor screen, the Family Hub pages and every test that mocks those paths keep working until the S10 cutover deletes the legacy UI. The wire-parity gates that read the client's wire types by path (`check-alliance-parity`, `check-behavioral-telemetry-parity`, `check-demo-step-parity`, `check-instrument-parity`, `check-session-end-parity` and two of their tests) now read `frontend/src/rebuild/mentor/session/types.ts`. No Oracle, Core or wire change; comments in `oracle/` that name the old client paths were left alone (this lane does not touch `oracle/`).

### W2M.1b: states as catalogue poses (08 §3, 02 §11 item 14)

The catalogue (`tutor-scene/poseLibrary.ts`, 100 rows, the maximum its own test allows) already covers every state, so no pose was added. The table (`rebuild/mentor/stageStates.ts`):

| State (08 §3) | Lively and moderate registers (6–9, 10–12) | Calm register (13–17, adult) |
|---|---|---|
| idle | `ambient.idle` (neutral, idle) | same |
| listening | `ambient.listen` (encouraging, idle) | same |
| thinking | `think.ponder` (thinking, think): a thinking pose, never typing dots | `teach.aside` (thinking, held low) |
| speaking | `ambient.idle.happy` (happy, idle); the renderer layers the speaking articulation and, with a voice clip, the mouth | same |
| demonstrating | `teach.explain` (encouraging, point toward the board) | same |
| encouraging | `feedback.retry.gentle` (encouraging, nod) | `ambient.listen` (encouraging, still) |
| celebrating (D7 only) | `celebrate.with` (encouraging, celebrate) | `celebrate.applaud` (proud, wave) |
| closing (C.16) | completed `transition.close.warm` (bow); interrupted and learner left `transition.exit` (wave); safety stop `transition.pause` (neutral, still, no praise) | same |
| acknowledging (08 §11) | `feedback.correct.quiet` (happy, still) | `greet.nod` (neutral, nod) |

`acknowledging` is not a ninth state of the Mentor screen: it is 08 §11's "neutral gesture" when a lesson answer is met, which the compact stage already showed (a quiet happy pose for children, a nod for teens and adults) and which none of the eight §3 states describes. The reactions are byte-for-byte what the compact stage did before (`mentorReaction` now reads this table; the S05.3f wellbeing test is unchanged and green). A celebration requested without a closed-list milestone (`design/milestones.ts`) is refused and shown as `idle`: the stage never fakes a state.

### W2M.1c: the stage (08 §2 layer 2, §6, §7)

**What the legacy code did.** The legacy Mentor screen composes `TutorStage` inside `tutor/stage/StageShell.tsx`, with a HUD, a microphone orb, a caption and chips laid over a full-screen canvas; the lesson's compact band (`learning/CompactMentorStage.tsx`) was a second, separate composition of `TutorStage`, with its own reduced-motion and low-power rules (a still for Dina only; the other three characters stayed 3D even on a low-power device).

**What changed.** `rebuild/mentor/MentorStage.tsx` is the one stage. Its documented interface:

- inputs: `character`, `state`, `board` (open or closed), `ageBand`; plus `size` (`full` or `compact`), `theme`, `scene` (`diorama-a` or `diorama-b`), `milestone` (required for a celebration), `closing` (the C.16 script), `beat` (plays the same state's gesture again), `shot` (from the session's phase), `copy` (its accessible name; without it the stage is decorative), and the voice clip (`speechUrl`, `audioKey`, `onSpeechEnd`, `onSpeechBlocked`: the renderer drives the mouth from it);
- outputs: `onReady({ mode, fallback, firstRenderMs, withinBudget })` once the character is on screen (the live model, or the still in a fallback), and `onError({ reason })` when the renderer failed (the stage then shows its still) or when no still exists for a fallback (the stage then shows its empty scene colour, never a stand-in).

How it renders is one pure decision (`stageMode.ts`): `live` (real-time 3D); `held` (the same 3D with poses held, for `prefers-reduced-motion`); `still` (no WebGL, the renderer's device probe starting at its lowest tier, data saver on, a renderer error, or three consecutive one-second readings under 30 fps at the renderer's lowest tier). The still of the same character shows from the first frame until the model is ready and cross-fades out (07 §4: the manifest's own renders; the lesson band stills where they exist, otherwise the character's transparent avatar render). A still shows the idle pose, and the stage says so (`data-still-pose`) rather than claiming the requested state. The first render is timed from mount and exposed (`data-first-render-ms`, `onReady`). The live stage claims the hero object's idle slot (02 §9.4). Under reduced motion a pose change fades the scene down, switches the held pose while it is faded and fades it back (150 ms each way); nothing travels. With the board open on a stage narrower than the desktop split, the scene renders in the part of the stage the board leaves free (`--lf-mentor-board-cover`, default 40%), so the camera frames the whole character above it; cropping the scene instead cut off the character's head (seen in the captures). On the desktop split nothing moves.

**Shot.** By default the full stage uses the `closeup` shot and the compact band keeps `closeup-wide` (its look before this change). Looking at the four characters on both Dioramas at 375 px, the wide close-up on `diorama-a` puts a rock prop in front of the character's legs and hands, which 08 §2 forbids ("Nothing decorative covers the character's face or hands"); the close-up frames all four clear of every prop. The Mentor screen will pass `shotForPhase`'s shot.

**Layout by width (preview only in W2M.1).** `MentorStagePreview.tsx` (`rebuild.html?screen=mentor-stage`) places the stage in the Mentor screen's regions: 58% of the height on a phone, half on a tablet (600–1023 px), the left 7 of 12 columns on a desktop, never smaller than the response area. The other region only names the character and what the stage is showing; the speech plate, board and response area are the next checkpoint's.

### W2M.1d: the compact stage is the same component (08 §10 item 9, §11)

`CompactMentorStage` is now `<MentorStage size="compact">`: the register's band height on a phone (110, 96 or 80 px), the lesson layout's 4-of-12 side column on a wide container (kept in `learning/mentorStage.css`), the lesson verdict mapped through `lessonStateFor` (no verdict: idle; met: acknowledging; any miss: encouraging). It is decorative (`aria-hidden`): the lesson's prompt label carries the Mentor's name and words. Three changes a family can see: all four characters now get a still on a low-power device (before, only Dina; the others stayed 3D on the device least able to run it); reduced motion now holds the character's pose in 3D, so the lesson's reaction stays visible (before, Dina became an idle still and the other three kept animating); and a repeated verdict can replay its gesture (`beat`). Lane 2's three tests that asserted `aria-label="zara"` on the band now assert `data-mentor-character` and mock the renderer's device probe, since the stage (correctly) falls back to a still in jsdom, which has no WebGL.

### W2M.1e: reduced motion in the engine (shared file, minimal change)

Before this change the engine honoured `prefers-reduced-motion` for the camera, the ambient bob and the speaking articulation, but still played every gesture and looped the idle clip. `characterActions.ts` gains `HELD_ACTION_PROGRESS` (one representative frame per action: the peak of a gesture's arc, the rest frame for `jump` and `hop` so nobody hangs in the air), and `Character3D.tsx` uses it when `settings.reducedMotion` is set: the procedural driver and the authored clips are held at that frame, the emotion layer is paused, the emotion posture's clock stops, and no lift is applied. The low tier alone does not hold poses (it is a performance decision; a gesture is how the learner sees what the character does). This also changes the legacy Mentor screen and the legacy lesson engine under reduced motion, in the direction 04 §3 asks. `TutorStage.tsx` forwards `onStats` (the renderer's once-a-second counters) so the stage can keep its 30 fps floor. Both are `tutor-scene/` files, which no lane owns; they are listed here for the orchestrator's merge.

## Remaining limitations and open items

- **Evidence scope.** Chromium only, local, the renderer on Chrome's software GL (SwiftShader) on a 32 GB / 16-thread Windows machine shared with four other lanes' browser matrices (CPU at about 70%). No real Core, database, Oracle or provider was contacted (OD-23). No device, Safari, Firefox or screen-reader pass (02 §12) and no human design review. Nothing here is accepted or released.
- **The rebuild audit on the real lesson route is owed** for the final code (see the verification log): rerun `audit:rebuild` with the lane's states and Lane 2's `app:/learn/lesson@allocation-adult` on a quiet machine.
- **Budgets not measured on the target device.** 08 §7's 2.5 s first render and 30 fps floor are for a mid-range phone. Here the first render measured about 1.0–2.1 s from a warm cache, and the software renderer drew 7–13 fps, so the stage took its frame-rate still within seconds, as designed. A device pass on real phones is required before the budgets can be called met (02 §11 item 15).
- **Per-state stills do not exist yet.** The fallback shows each character's idle render (and Dina's lesson band stills): the layout and the character are right, the state is not visible on the character in the still fallback. Rendering the eight states for four characters in two modes (64 renders through the pose-lab pipeline, `scripts/render-mentor-avatars.mjs`, about four minutes each in software) and registering them under a `mentor.stageStill` slot is asset work for the character-render family, which still awaits the owner's first-family style review (07 §7). `stageStills.ts` already prefers such a still when one is registered.
- **A changed reduced-motion preference applies on reload.** The renderer reads the preference once per page (its device probe is cached), so the stage reads it the same way rather than claim a held mode the engine is not in.
- **Found by looking, not fixed: Diorama props can cross the character.** With the close-up, Dr. Rho's thinking pose tilts his head down behind the ammonite on `diorama-a`, and on `diorama-b` a bush can stand in front of Dina. 08 §2 forbids anything decorative covering the face or hands. The props and the standing spot are placed by the engine (`tutor-scene/Diorama.tsx`, `standingSpots.ts`, `culling.ts`) and the legacy screen shows the same; the fix (keep props out of the camera-to-character corridor for the stage's shots) belongs with the Mentor screen checkpoint and changes the legacy screen too.
- **The full-size still is the avatar render**, a head-and-shoulders crop made for the 96 px avatar slot: on the full stage it reads as a very large head. A full-body stage still is part of the per-state stills work below.
- **The idle slot claim cannot stop the engine's idle loop.** If another hero idle loop held the slot, the stage records that it did not get it (`data-idle-motion` absent) but the live model still breathes; the Mentor screen and the lesson have no other hero loop.
- **The board is a layout contract only.** The stage reframes above an open board; the board itself, its size on each width and its entrance are the Mentor screen's.
- **The Mentor route is still the legacy screen.** `/tutor` renders `routes/app/TutorPage.tsx` and the legacy `TutorExperience`, now on the ported session layer. The rebuilt Mentor screen (08 §2 layers 1, 3–5, the transcript sheet, the chooser of 08 §8, "what my grown-up sees", the microphone per C.2) is the next checkpoint, and with it the route switch.
- **Synthetic Core.** The lane answers `GET /learn/register` for its own scenarios; the shared audit fixtures carry only the adult allocation document, so the audit measures the compact stage for the adult learner and the lane's own matrix builds each band's document.
- **Draft assets in product use.** The stills are the manifest's draft Mentor renders (07 §7); a release build refuses drafts by design (S03).
