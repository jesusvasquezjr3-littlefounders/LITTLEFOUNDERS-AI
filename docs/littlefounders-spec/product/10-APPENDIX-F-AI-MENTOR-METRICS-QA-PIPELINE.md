# Appendix F — AI Mentor Success Metrics, Definition of Done, QA & Deployment Pipeline

**Status:** Authoritative operational framework closing Block C. Appendix D answers what the AI Mentor should decide, live, turn by turn. Appendix E answers how the Mentor is allowed to improve itself over time, and under what governance. This appendix answers the three questions still open after C.1–C.24 and the Real-Time Interaction Standard were written: **how do we know it's actually working, how do we know a given requirement is done, and how does a change to a live conversational system get built, tested, and released end-to-end** without drifting into the same engagement-optimization risk Appendix C was built to guard against in authored content — except here the risk is live and conversational, which makes it both harder to catch and more consequential when missed. Every metric and gate below ties back to a specific requirement (C.x) or research finding (Appendix D/E §x) so this framework is traceable, not free-floating. Where a metric already exists in `10-APPENDIX-C-SUCCESS-METRICS-QA-PIPELINE.md` (Session Efficiency Ratio, AI Mentor Resolution Efficiency), it is referenced, not duplicated.

## Part 1 — Success Metrics Framework

Four categories, because a real-time conversational system can fail in more distinct ways than authored content can: it can be pedagogically sound but relationally cold, relationally warm but pedagogically dishonest (sycophantic), safe on paper but unsafe in the specific accounts that fall through a role-based gap, or well-designed but poorly governed as it evolves. No single category may be used alone to declare Block C "working."

### 1.1 Real-Time Pedagogical Effectiveness Metrics

| Metric | What it measures | Data source | Target | Verifies |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mastery Declaration Reversal Rate | % of "mastered" knowledge-component declarations later demoted under the Khan-Academy-style demotion rule (Appendix D §2.3), within a rolling 90-day post-mastery window (proposed provisional window, pending calibration data) | Extended Mastery Engine event log | Establish release-1 baseline; trend toward low single digits — a rising rate signals premature-mastery risk before it becomes visible in downstream course performance | C.10 |
| Corroborating-Evidence Compliance Rate | % of mastery-declaration or remediation/rescue triggers that had ≥2 consecutive qualifying observations before firing | Extended Mastery Engine event log | 100% — this is a hard controller invariant, not a soft target; any exception is a bug, not a tuning choice | C.10 |
| Hint-Ladder Escalation Integrity | % of hint sequences with zero level-repeats, and % of "just tell me" requests honored on the same turn requested | Dialogue-manager event log | 100% on both sub-measures | C.13 |

### 1.2 Relational & Affective Health Metrics

This category exists because Appendix D Part 3.7 documents, with direct evidence, that the behaviors which make a learner *say* a session felt good (unconditional praise, no pushback, always resolving confusion smoothly) can be the same behaviors that produce worse learning outcomes. These metrics are designed so that a comfortable-feeling session is not automatically treated as a successful one.

| Metric | What it measures | Data source | Target | Verifies |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alliance Bond Proxy Score | Aggregated result of the lightweight end-of-session check ("did I get what you were going for today?"), tracked per learner and per mentor persona | Alliance Controller session-close event | Diagnostic — no fixed target; establish baseline per persona, and a persona scoring meaningfully below the others triggers a review of that persona's dialogue calibration. See the Part 3 Stage 7 kill-switch trigger (a >15% relative drop from established baseline, proposed) for the automatic-rollback threshold built on this same signal | C.15 |
| Goal-Agreement Completion Rate | % of sessions (above a minimal turn-count floor) with an explicit, completed session-opening goal-agreement move | Alliance Controller event log | Near 100% for sessions clearing the turn-count floor | C.15 |
| Adaptation-Offer Renegotiation Trigger Rate | % of sessions where a defined pattern of repeated adaptation-offer declines correctly produced a renegotiation move rather than silent persistence | Alliance Controller event log | Diagnostic — used to confirm the mechanism fires in practice, not just in the spec | C.15 |
| Answer-Reveal Rate | % of Mentor turns, during a collaborative-repair or hint-ladder sequence, that reveal a solution rather than scaffold toward it — modeled on the MathDial methodology (Appendix D §3.7) | Sampled transcript scoring (calibrated judge, per 1.3, plus human spot-check) | Establish an acceptable ceiling from a human-rated baseline batch; any upward drift is treated as a controller defect, never a neutral helpfulness signal | C.18 |
| Sycophancy Audit Score | Sampled-transcript audit rate of praise tied to specific, verifiable learner actions vs. generic affirmation; zero-tolerance check for affirming an unsound in-scenario financial decision | Sampled transcript audit | Zero tolerance on the unsound-decision-affirmation check; trend toward specific-over-generic praise on the general measure | C.18 |
| Disengagement-Repair Initiation Rate | % of fired disengagement signals (from the Behavioral Telemetry Layer) that produced the required explicit check-in move | Behavioral Telemetry Layer + dialogue-manager event log | 100% — a hard controller invariant | C.19 |
| Default-to-Inaction Rate | % of turns where the Behavioral Telemetry Layer computes a signal but takes no adaptation-offer action | Behavioral Telemetry Layer event log | High, by design (per Appendix D §1.7) — most turns should trigger nothing; a low rate signals over-intervention risk, itself a bitter-aftertaste risk. See the Part 3 Stage 7 kill-switch floor (85%, proposed) for the automatic-rollback threshold | C.9 |
| Session-Closing Script Accuracy | % of sessions closed using the correct end-reason-specific script (completed / budget-reached / learner-left / safety-stop) | Session-close event log | 100% | C.16 |
| Age-Band Calibration A/B Outcome | A/B comparison of the Alliance Bond Proxy Score (above) and Session-Closing Script outcomes (peak-end sentiment at close) between learners receiving age-band-differentiated hint pacing/scaffolding (C.17) and a control variant, segmented by age tier | Alliance Controller + session-close event logs, segmented by age-band calibration variant | Statistically significant improvement, or at minimum non-regression, in alliance-bond and session-closing telemetry for the age-band-calibrated variant vs. control — this fulfills C.17's own mandate that the design decision be A/B-tested and directly measured via C.15–C.16 telemetry, not assumed correct on adoption | C.17 |

## Part 2 — Definition of Done

### 2.1 Generic Definition of Done (applies to every item C.1–C.24)

Mirrors the standard set in Appendix C Part 2.1, adapted for a real-time conversational system. A requirement is not "done" when code merges. It is done when all four of the following are true:

1. **Functional** — the described behavior is implemented and a test reproducing the exact scenario in the requirement's "Current State" now passes (for defects) or the new capability is exercised end-to-end (for new capability), including at least one live or simulated conversational session that exercises it.
2. **Gated** — where the requirement introduces or modifies a real-time controller behavior, it is exercised by the relevant persona(s) in the simulated-student adversarial suite (Part 3, Stage 2), and demonstrably produces the correct behavior against a deliberately adversarial scenario built to fail it — not just a happy-path conversation.
3. **Measured** — at least one metric from Part 1 is instrumented and reporting real production or canary data tied to this requirement within one release cycle of shipping. A requirement with no corresponding data point one release later is not verifiably done, regardless of code state. Reporting data alone is not sufficient: for any such metric not explicitly labeled "Diagnostic" in Part 1 (i.e., any metric with a stated fixed target, hard invariant, or directional trend target), the metric must also be trending toward — or, for a hard invariant, already meeting — its stated target before this criterion is satisfied. A metric that is merely present and flat, or moving away from its target, does not satisfy "Measured," regardless of how long it has been instrumented. A metric explicitly labeled "Diagnostic" in Part 1 is exempt from this trend requirement by design — it exists to surface information, not to be driven toward a fixed number.
4. **Reviewed** — for a Tier 1 item (per Appendix E §3.1, C.22), both a Pedagogical Reviewer and a Safety/Trust Lead sign off against the specific finding in the requirement; for a Tier 2 item, the calibrated judge's pass plus a human spot-check of the canary sample; for a Tier 3 item, the Pedagogical Lead confirms the metric is live and visible on the dashboard (C.24).

### 2.2 Worked examples — the three highest-ambiguity items

**C.9 (Behavioral Telemetry Layer):** Done when (a) all signals specified in Appendix D §1.3–1.4 are computed from existing conversational/interaction data with no new data collection, (b) the layer never outputs a declarative emotion label — verified by a dedicated adversarial test asserting no such output exists across the full simulated-student suite, (c) the Default-to-Inaction Rate (Part 1.2) is instrumented and reporting within one release cycle, and (d) a kill-switch threshold (Part 3, Stage 7) is defined and tested before the layer's output is allowed to drive any adaptation-offer action in production.

**C.15 (Alliance Controller):** Done when (a) bond/goal/task-agreement state is tracked per session as explicit, queryable data — not inferred only implicitly by the pedagogy controller, (b) the goal-agreement opening move and the renegotiation trigger on repeated declines are both exercised and pass in the simulated-student suite, (c) persona-continuity re-establishment is verified with a test scenario that deliberately switches personas mid-relationship, and (d) the Alliance Bond Proxy Score (Part 1.2) is live and segmentable by persona within one release cycle.

**C.22 (tiered governance model):** Done when (a) the three-tier classification (never-automate / gated-automate / fully-automate) is written down as an internal policy document with named examples of what falls in each tier, (b) the Tier-Compliance Audit (Part 1.3) exists and is run at least once against current AI Mentor architecture before any automated proposal-generation capability is built, and (c) every subsequent Block C item touching automation (C.5, C.21, C.23, C.24) explicitly states which tier it operates under in its own documentation — this item is a governance decision, not a code deliverable, and its Definition of Done reflects that.

## Part 3 — Real-Time QA & Deployment Pipeline

This is the answer to "how does a change to the AI Mentor's live behavior get built, tested, and released without drifting into ambiguity or unreviewed risk." Unlike Appendix C's lesson pipeline, every stage here must account for the fact that the system being changed is talking to a child in real time — a bad authored lesson is caught on review before anyone sees it; a bad Mentor-behavior change can play out mid-conversation before anyone notices.

**Stage 0 — Scoping & Risk-Tier Classification** (Pedagogical Lead + Safety/Trust Lead, human)
Before any change is authored, it is classified against the Tier 1/2/3 model (C.22, Appendix E §3.1): does this touch the safety judge, the core evaluation rubric, or anything monetization-adjacent (Tier 1, full human review only, never entering an automated path)? Is it a prompt/pacing/persona micro-variant within already-approved bounds (Tier 2, gated-automate eligible)? Is it measurement/flagging only (Tier 3, fully-automate eligible)? This classification determines which of the following stages are mandatory versus skippable for this specific change.

**Stage 1 — Development** (Engineering / Pedagogical Lead, human-authored or AI-assisted proposal)
The change is authored — a new hint-ladder rule, a persona tone adjustment, a new disengagement-response script. AI-assisted proposal generation is explicitly permitted for Tier 2 and Tier 3 changes per Appendix E §2.3, but every proposal enters Stage 2 exactly like a human-authored one — no exemption based on who or what produced it.

**Stage 2 — Simulated-Student Adversarial Testing** (automated)
The change is run against a defined suite of simulated learner personas built from Appendix D's own research: a frustrated-but-engaged learner (Pekrun's high-value/low-control profile), a bored/disengaging learner (the D'Mello/Graesser "sink state"), a gaming-the-system learner (Baker's rapid-guessing pattern), a reactant teen (Appendix D §3.6's autonomy-threat profile), and a masking/anxious learner (Appendix D §1.6's developmental-masking profile). A change that degrades behavior against any one persona returns to Stage 1 with an itemized failure report — never a generic rejection.

**Stage 3 — Calibrated-Judge Scoring** (automated, Tier 2 changes only; skipped for Tier 3, mandatory-bypassed to Stage 4 for Tier 1)
The change's simulated-student transcripts are scored by the AI judge calibrated per C.23. A judge that has not been calibrated, or whose calibration has gone stale per the Judge–Human Agreement Rate (Part 1.3), may not gate this stage — an uncalibrated judge routes the change directly to Stage 4 as if it were Tier 1.

**Stage 4 — Human Pedagogical + Safety Review** (Pedagogical Reviewer + Safety/Trust Lead, human, distinct roles even when frequently filled by people who also do Stage 1 work, to avoid a false sense of independent review)
Mandatory for every Tier 1 change, and for any Tier 2 change that failed or was ambiguous at Stage 3. Reviews specifically for what Stages 2–3 cannot judge: does this actually preserve the Working Alliance framing (C.15)? Does it risk the "sabor amargo" outcome in a way no adversarial persona captured? A review that says only "approved" without addressing the specific risk this change was scoped against does not satisfy Part 2's Definition of Done.

**Stage 5 — Canary Rollout** (Engineering + Pedagogical Lead)
A small percentage of real sessions receive the change first. A human samples and reads real transcripts from this population before full release — this is not optional even for a Tier 2 change that passed Stages 2–4 cleanly, because simulated personas and judge scoring are both, per Appendix D §1.7 and Appendix E §1.2, imperfect proxies for real behavior.

**Stage 6 — Full Release & Instrumentation** (Engineering)
The change ships with its relevant Part 1 metric already instrumented — as in Appendix C, metrics are a launch requirement, not a follow-up ticket.

**Stage 7 — Post-Launch Monitoring & Kill-Switch Governance** (Pedagogical Lead + Safety/Trust Lead, on the cadence defined in the Threshold Recalibration Log, Part 1.4)
Every new real-time component carries an explicit, pre-defined automatic-rollback condition — the concrete operationalization of Baker's "Stupid Tutoring Systems, Intelligent Humans" principle already cited in Appendix D §2.6 (automation should be simple and human-overridable, not maximally sophisticated and irreversible):

| Component | Kill-switch trigger | Rollback action |
|---|---|---|
| Behavioral Telemetry Layer | Default-to-Inaction Rate (Part 1.2) drops below 85% (proposed provisional floor, pending calibration data), or the Disengagement-Repair Initiation Rate (Part 1.2) drops below 100% in a sampled audit | Disable adaptation-offer triggering from this layer; revert to time/turn-cap-only session management (the pre-C.9 baseline) until root-caused |
| Extended Mastery Engine | Mastery Declaration Reversal Rate (Part 1.1) exceeds 8% (proposed provisional ceiling, pending calibration data), or the Corroborating-Evidence Compliance Rate drops below 100% | Revert to single-observation BKT thresholds (the pre-C.10 baseline) for affected knowledge components until root-caused |
| Alliance Controller | Alliance Bond Proxy Score for any persona drops by more than 15% relative to its established per-persona baseline (proposed provisional threshold, pending calibration data), or the Renegotiation Trigger fires with no measurable session-quality improvement over a rolling sample of the most recent 50 renegotiation-trigger events (proposed provisional sample size, pending calibration data) | Suspend the renegotiation-trigger and persona-continuity re-establishment behaviors; retain passive bond/goal tracking (measurement only) until root-caused |
| Live-Generation Content Judge (C.5, C.6) | Judge Approval-Quality Concordance Rate (Part 1.3) drops below 90% (proposed provisional floor, pending calibration data) against the post-hoc human sample for any content-risk category, or the human-reviewed sample rate for that category falls below the Appendix E §3.1.1 floor (15% standard / 50% sensitive-topic-flagged content) | Suspend autonomous judge approval for live-generation content in the affected risk category; route requests to the curated-activity-pack tier (C.6) or a human-authored fallback until the judge is recalibrated per Appendix E §3.2 |

Every provisional floor, ceiling, threshold, sample size, and cadence value in the table above is marked "proposed, pending calibration" deliberately: none has yet been validated against real production data, and each must be reviewed on the same quarterly cadence as every other threshold in the Threshold Recalibration Log (Part 1.4) once real production data exists to calibrate it against — none of these provisional values should be read as final. Real production data from this stage feeds back into the Threshold Recalibration Log exactly as in Appendix C Part 1.3/Stage 6 — every numeric threshold in this appendix and in Appendices D/E is a research-grounded starting point, not a permanent constant.

## A note on why this appendix exists

Everything in C.1–C.24 describes *what* must be true of the AI Mentor's real-time behavior and its governance. Without this appendix, a development team would have had to invent its own answer to "how do we know this is working," "what counts as done," and "in what order do we build 24 interdependent real-time components" — under exactly the kind of time pressure that, per Appendix E Part 1.2, is when reward-hacking-prone shortcuts and ungated automation get built first and governed later, if ever. The kill-switch table in Part 3 Stage 7 and the Tier-Compliance Audit in Part 1.3 exist specifically so that "the Mentor got better at engaging students" is never, by itself, treated as evidence that Block C succeeded — exactly the standard Appendix C Part 1.2 already set for authored content, now extended to the live conversation itself.
